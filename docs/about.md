# Mission

> **No one should die of a disease we could have understood in time.**

That sentence is the whole reason this project exists, and it is the test every decision here has to
pass. When a technical choice, a design flourish, or a marketing line conflicts with the mission — or
with telling the truth about what is and isn't real yet — the mission wins.

## Why a factory

For too many families, the hard part was never that the answer was unknowable — it was that it
arrived too late, cost too much, or lived behind a wall. The HCLS AI Factory is an attempt to
compress the distance between a patient's biology and an understanding a clinician can act on: from
raw DNA toward a shortlist of therapeutic candidates in hours instead of months, on **one open box
anyone can run**, bursting heavier work to remote GPUs only when it needs to.

It is open-source under Apache-2.0 — free to use, free to build on, commercial use welcome — because
the fastest way to help the next child is to let anyone, anywhere, run and improve the tools.

## Held to the truth

This is a bench, not a cure. It **supports a qualified clinician; it does not diagnose, prescribe, or
treat.** Where the work points toward gene therapies, those are **preclinical** research — the promise
of a direction, not a treatment available today. Everything the site claims is
[rendered from the code](honesty/maturity-matrix.md), so what you read is what actually exists.

We would rather promise less and mean all of it. The honest version of this project is the only
version worth building — and the only one that earns the trust of the clinicians, scientists, and
families it is for.

## The first child

The flagship program is [**Tuberous Sclerosis Complex**](programs/tsc.md) — a multi-system condition
that touches brain, kidney, heart, skin, and lungs, and a clean gene-to-drug story. It is where the
whole factory converges on one patient, in one governed afternoon: a real, approved therapy today,
and an open bench for what comes next. Told plainly, without montage — because a quiet true sentence
about a child is worth more than any spectacle, and honesty here is not just ethics, it is the point.
