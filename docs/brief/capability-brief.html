HCLS AI Factory · Capability Brief

Patient DNA to therapeutic candidates, in hours — on one box you can fork.

No one should die of a disease we could have understood in time.

The HCLS AI Factory is an open-source precision-medicine platform that runs on a single NVIDIA DGX Spark and compresses the work of an entire translational team — raw sequencing reads to a shortlist of therapeutic candidates — from months into hours. It is organized as eight engines (the horizontal compute muscle), eight intelligence agents (the clinical reasoning layers that interpret what the engines produce), and disease programs (verticals that compose all of them for one condition, starting with Tuberous Sclerosis Complex). It runs “one box, elastic burst”: heavy or ARM-incompatible models burst to a remote GPU over a private mesh — nothing pretends to fit on the desk that doesn’t. And the line that makes it trustworthy: every clinical output is decision support for a qualified clinician — never autonomous diagnosis or prescribing.

The horizontal compute layer

The 8 Engines

Deterministic compute muscle — genomics, discovery, imaging, oncology, cardiology, protein design, single-cell.

E1

Genomic Foundation

:5000 (+8575)

The front door — reads to annotated variants

Turns raw sequencing reads into an annotated, clinically-framed list of variants using GPU-accelerated tools (Parabricks, DeepVariant, HaplotypeCaller) and reference knowledge bases (ClinVar, AlphaMissense), with quality controls, secondary-finding checks (ACMG SF), and mosaicism/allele-fraction analysis built in. For a family it reads the genome; for a geneticist it is a reproducible, guideline-anchored variant pipeline.

E2

Precision Intelligence

:5001

The interpreter over the genome

A retrieval-augmented reasoning layer that sits on top of the variant foundation, explains what a variant means in clinical context, and coordinates the eight agents — so a wall of variants becomes a prioritized, cited narrative. It is the difference between “here are 11 million variants” and “here is the one that matters, and why.”

E3

Therapeutic Discovery

:8505 (+8574, 8572)

Designs candidate small-molecule drugs

Once a target is identified, this engine designs candidate small molecules against it — generating molecules, docking them, scoring for drug-likeness and safety (ADMET), and iterating (generate → score → reseed), with co-folding via Chai-1. It is the “and then we designed a drug for it” moment made real.

Designs are computational leads for a bench and a medicinal chemist, not approved therapies. Chai-1 planned.

E4

Clinical Imaging

:8525

Reads scans — and reasons across genomics

Reads medical images (CT, MRI, X-ray) with vision models (VISTA-3D, VILA-M3), returns structured FHIR-ready findings, and — the powerful part — reasons across images and genomics together, so a scan and a genome inform one conclusion.

Synthetic imaging (MAISI) is research / QA augmentation only — never a diagnostic source.

Precision Oncology

:8526

Molecular tumor board — pediatric-first

Assembles molecular-tumor-board packets — ranking therapies against a tumor’s mutations and matching patients to clinical trials — with pediatric oncology built in as a first-class, different case (fusion-first, not adult logic bent to fit a child). Every packet is decision support for a tumor board, never an autonomous treatment decision.

Cardiology

Prevention → intervention → rhythm

A cardiovascular decision-support engine spanning 11 clinical workflows and 6 validated risk calculators (including coronary-calcium and ASCVD scoring), with genomic triggers for inherited heart conditions. It is where a coronary-calcium score, a genome, and a statin recommendation meet.

The protein-design suite

Single-Cell Analysis

:8573

The compute base for single-cell

Does the deterministic single-cell number-crunching (via scanpy) — clustering cells by type and mapping the tumor microenvironment — so downstream reasoning stands on real computed clusters. The clinical reasoning layer

The 8 Intelligence Agents

Reasoning layers that interpret what the engines compute — each decision support for a qualified clinician.

CAR-T Intelligence

:8521

Cell therapy

Reasons across the CAR-T literature, trials, and construct designs — running comparative analyses (e.g., one costimulatory domain vs. another) and deep evidence synthesis — to support how an engineered cell therapy is designed and evaluated.

D5

Precision Biomarker Intelligence

:8528

Biomarkers

Interprets biomarkers in a genotype-aware way and estimates biological age (PhenoAge/GrimAge) across a nine-domain risk picture, joining multi-omics signals into one read. It turns scattered markers into “here’s what your biology is actually saying.”

Pharmacogenomics Intelligence

:8507

Drug–gene

Calls star-alleles from the genome and applies CPIC guidelines and dosing algorithms to flag drug–gene risks before a prescription — a genuine pre-prescription safety interlock (statins, thiopurines, and more). It flags and recommends; it never prescribes.

Precision Autoimmune Intelligence

:8531

Autoimmune

Interprets autoantibodies and HLA typing and reasons about flare risk for immune-mediated conditions, giving a specialist a structured, evidence-linked view of an autoimmune picture.

Neurology Intelligence

:8529

Neurology

Spans stroke triage, dementia evaluation, and multiple-sclerosis scoring (EDSS), with staged Parkinson’s assessment featured — decision support across the neurology suite.

Newer staging frameworks (e.g., αSyn-SAA-based) are research / trial-use inputs, not routine diagnostics.
D7

Clinical Trial Intelligence

:8128

Clinical trials

Optimizes trial design and biomarker strategy and matches patients to trials — the agent that, at a demo’s close, takes a freshly discovered molecule or a diagnosed child and asks “what trial does this open?” It is the bridge from discovery back to a patient’s next real option.

Rare disease

The Showcase

Tuberous Sclerosis Complex

Where all eight engines and eight agents converge on a single child.

TSC is the factory’s flagship disease program and the demonstration that shows why all of the above is built as one system rather than eighteen clever tools. It is a rare genetic disorder where loss of a single gene — TSC1 or TSC2 — releases a growth-control brake (the mTORC1 pathway), causing benign tumors to grow across many organs at once: brain, kidneys, heart, skin, and lungs. That multi-system reach is exactly why it lights up the whole factory — genomics, imaging, oncology-style tumor tracking, and specialist reasoning all converge on the same child — and it comes with a clean, textbook gene-to-drug story.

TSC1 / TSC2 loss  →  mTORC1 hyperactivation  →  everolimus  (FDA-approved: SEGA 2010 · renal AML 2012 · seizures 2018)

On disk it is a self-contained vertical (core/disease-programs/): a dedicated TSC engine plus five disease-specific agents that compose the eight horizontal engines and eight agents for this one condition rather than reinventing them.

· Variant Curator · Trajectory Modeler · Therapeutics Strategist · Phenome Mapper · TAND Surveillance

The D3 demonstration walks a single infant through the factory in one governed afternoon, built as the project’s signature three-beat arc:

Beat 01

Weight

A diagnosis that historically means a years-long odyssey across a half-dozen specialists.

Beat 02

Compression

The factory reads the genome, finds the causative TSC variant, maps organ involvement, and surfaces the mTOR-inhibitor rationale — in hours.

Beat 03

Hope

A cited, decision-ready picture for the clinician — and the child’s de-identified data handed forward so the next child’s afternoon is faster.

Honesty · TherapyEverolimus is a real, approved therapy for TSC — but TSC1/TSC2 gene therapy is preclinical. The factory is the open design-and-analysis bench, not a cure today.
Honesty · UseEvery output is decision support for the clinician and family — never an autonomous diagnosis — with pediatric caution held at full force.

TSC is the first vertical, not the last: the same pattern is designed to replicate to NF1, NF2, Rett, Williams, and the broader mTORopathies — same foundation, same arc.
