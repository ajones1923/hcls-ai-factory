# Capability Brief — Mission Cut

*The technical picture is [one click away](README.md). This is the why.*

---

For too many families, the hard part was never that the answer was unknowable. It was that it
arrived too late, cost too much, or lived behind a wall someone else owned.

The HCLS AI Factory is a small, stubborn attempt to move that wall. It takes a patient's own
biology — their DNA — and compresses the distance to an understanding a clinician can act on: from
raw reads toward a shortlist of candidate therapies in **hours** instead of months. It runs on **one
open box** anyone can buy, fork, and improve, and it is free — Apache-2.0, commercial use welcome —
because the fastest way to help the next child is to let anyone, anywhere, run the tools and make
them better.

It does not diagnose. It does not prescribe. It **supports a qualified clinician** — makes the
evidence legible and fast, so the human who carries the responsibility can carry it better. Where the
work points toward cures still on the bench — gene therapy for conditions like tuberous sclerosis —
we say **preclinical**, plainly, because false hope is its own kind of harm. We would rather promise
less and mean all of it.

The first child the factory is built around has [tuberous sclerosis complex](../programs/tsc.md) — a
condition that touches brain, kidney, heart, skin, and lungs at once. For that child there is a real,
approved medicine today, and an open bench for what comes next. That is the whole shape of the hope
here: **something true now, and a door held open for later.**

> **No one should die of a disease we could have understood in time.**

That sentence is the reason the project exists, and the test every decision has to pass. It is not a
slogan to put over a montage. It is a quiet promise — to be honest about what is real, generous with
what we build, and worthy of the people it is for.
