# Skeptics / Competitors — guaranteed, and louder the louder you are

Skeptics and competitors show up on every medical-AI launch. They are motivated to find the flaw
and name it: **"vaporware," "where's the peer review," "no one person could have built all this."**
They get louder in exact proportion to how loud the artifact is — a bigger claim is a bigger
target. You cannot avoid them; you can only decide whether they find ammunition.

## What wins them / What loses them

**Neutralized by:** openness and reproducibility. **"Fork it and run it yourself"** is the argument
that ends the vaporware fight — a critic cannot credibly call a thing fake when they can clone it
and watch it run on one affordable box. An open, Apache-2.0, reproducible repo turns the loudest
skeptic's best attack into a dead end.

**Empowered by:** any gap between claim and reality. Every unlabeled roadmap item, every "cure"
where you meant "candidate," every "diagnoses" where you meant "supports a clinician," every
"all on one box" that quietly needs a remote cluster — becomes their headline. The honesty ledger
is not caution for its own sake; it is the removal of their weapons.

## How to speak to them

- **Make openness the lead argument, not a footnote.** Point to the repo, the license, the
  one-command bring-up. Reproducibility is the response that pre-empts "vaporware."
- **Guard the honesty ledger hardest here.** Every claim must survive a hostile expert reading it
  in bad faith. If it can't be defended in front of a skeptic, cut or qualify it *before* they do.
- **Concede scope proactively.** Name what's preclinical, what's roadmap, what bursts to remote
  GPUs ("elastic burst," never all-local), and that outputs are decision support, not diagnosis.
  Stating your own limits leaves them nothing to "expose."
- On **"no one person did all this"**: lean into the Linux framing — a working foundation released
  open precisely so it doesn't depend on one author, with clean provenance and named tools
  (Parabricks, DeepVariant, MolMIM, DiffDock, ClinVar) anyone can verify.
- Match volume with **checkability, not superlatives.** The louder the artifact, the tighter the
  claims must be — because the scrutiny scales with the reach.

## Do / Don't

**Do:** lead with fork-it-yourself; pre-empt with honest scope; keep every claim defensible under
hostile reading; cite sources; name the burst/preclinical/roadmap pieces. **Don't:** overclaim,
dramatize past the ledger, hide a limitation, imply all-local, or let spectacle write a check the
repo can't cash — that check is their ammunition.
