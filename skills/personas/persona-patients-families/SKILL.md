# Patients / Families — the emotional core, the highest-stakes ground

Patients, families, and advocacy groups. They are the reason the project exists and the audience
most easily harmed by it. They care about hope that is *real* — and the mission line is theirs:
*no one should die of a disease we could have understood in time.* Restraint here is not tone
management; it is ethics and the protection of vulnerable people.

## What wins them / What loses them

**Wins them:** dignified, understated truth. Concrete, honest framing of what is possible **now**
versus **later** — a clinician getting a better-supported picture sooner, a research bench that
lets scientists explore candidate molecules faster, a cost low enough that a clinic without a
genetics department can still give a family an answer. Quiet honesty about limits reads as respect,
not weakness.

**Loses them / does harm:** raising hope the project cannot yet deliver. Implying "cure,"
"treatment available," or "this would have saved your child" hurts the very people the project is
for — and invites the two most damaging critiques a medical-AI project can attract: **"false hope"**
and **"using sick kids for marketing."** Over-promising here is not just a messaging miss; it is a
wound.

## How to speak to them

- Use the **mission cut** — understated, human, true. One quiet sentence about the stakes carries
  further than any montage and cannot be read as manipulation.
- Draw the **now-vs-later line explicitly and gently**: what helps a clinician or researcher today,
  what is still preclinical or roadmap. Never let a family infer a treatment exists because a
  molecule was generated. Candidate molecules are for researchers to test; outputs are decision
  support for a doctor, not a diagnosis or a promise.
- **Never use a patient — especially a child — as a prop.** If a real story is invoked, it is with
  consent, dignity, and no leap from their story to a claim the project can't back.
- Let openness carry the hope honestly: the point is that the tools are real and free, so more
  people can build toward answers — not that answers have arrived.
- When in doubt, **under-claim.** For this audience, the cost of overstating is measured in real
  human hurt; the cost of understating is only slightly less reach.

## Do / Don't

**Do:** be understated and true; separate now from later plainly; honor the mission line with
restraint; center dignity. **Don't:** promise cures, treatments, or outcomes; imply a therapy
exists; instrumentalize a sick patient; let spectacle outrun what is real for someone who is
desperate to believe it.
